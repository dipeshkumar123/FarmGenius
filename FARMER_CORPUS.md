## 3. Existing App Failure Modes

Based on research into farmer feedback regarding existing AgriTech solutions in India (Plantix, KisanVaani, AgriApp), the following are the top 10 failure modes and complaints:

1. **High Connectivity Dependence:** Apps require high-speed internet to upload images for disease diagnostics. They fail entirely in rural 2G zones or fields with no signal, which is where they are needed most.
2. **Complex UI & Digital Literacy Barriers:** Heavy reliance on text-based navigation and typing makes apps inaccessible to functionally illiterate or digitally hesitant farmers. There is a lack of robust voice-first integration.
3. **Inaccurate Multimodal Diagnostics:** Diagnostic models often fail on complex real-world scenarios—such as a plant suffering from both a pest attack and a nutrient deficiency simultaneously, yielding an incorrect "primary" diagnosis.
4. **Pushing Agrochemical Sales:** Farmers frequently complain that app recommendations are biased towards selling specific, expensive chemical brands rather than suggesting localized, generic, or organic interventions.
5. **Formal Language / Dialect Mismatch:** Apps utilize formal textbook translations (e.g., highly sanskritized Hindi or pure formal Kannada) that do not match the local colloquialisms or dialect terms actually used by farmers (e.g., "Jhulsa" vs formal agricultural jargon).
6. **Abstract "Theoretical" Advice:** Diagnoses often feel disconnected from reality. Recommending a specific niche chemical that is entirely unavailable at the local rural input dealer makes the advice useless.
7. **Single-Crop Bias:** Apps often focus exclusively on the primary cash crop, failing to support the multi-crop, mixed-farming ecosystems that most smallholder Indian farmers maintain.
8. **Outdated/Inaccurate Pricing:** Mandi prices aggregated on apps often lag by 24-48 hours or do not reflect the specific sub-market the farmer actually sells at, leading to trust erosion.
9. **Expert Forum "Ghosting":** Community QA features promise expert agronomist replies, but questions frequently go unanswered for days. Since pests destroy fields in 48 hours, slow responses are functionally useless.
10. **Device Strain:** Large application sizes (>50MB) and high battery drain cause frequent uninstallations on the low-end Android smartphones prevalent in rural India.

## 4. Trust Signals

To build an application that a smallholder farmer will actually follow, the system must trigger specific sociopsychological "trust signals." Research into KVKs (Krishi Vigyan Kendras) and local agronomy structures reveals:

*   **Accountability & Local Presence:** Farmers heavily favor local agronomists because there is a physical person to hold accountable. If an intervention fails, the farmer can visit them. An app is perceived as an anonymous "black box." To mitigate this, an app must transparently cite its sources (e.g., "According to Dharwad KVK...").
*   **Demonstrability (Seeing is Believing):** Indian farmers prioritize visual proof over digital promises. While local experts can show physical field results, an app must rely on hyper-localized case studies or clear, realistic visual comparisons.
*   **Hyper-Local Nuance over Generalization:** Farmers instantly dismiss broad advice (e.g., "sow wheat in November"). They trust advisors who understand their specific district's micro-climate and soil type. Contextual awareness (knowing the farmer's exact district) is a mandatory trust signal.
*   **Timeliness & Problem-First Interactions:** Farmers usually only seek advice when there is an immediate, critical problem. Fast, direct, and urgently relevant responses build trust faster than comprehensive (but slow) educational content.
*   **Social Proof via "Village Champions":** Technology is rarely adopted in isolation. Apps are trusted when endorsed by a "progressive farmer" or local extension worker (e.g., Krishi Sakhi) who provides initial handholding and validates the tool's effectiveness.
*   **Linguistic Empathy:** Using regional dialects and conversational tones makes the tool feel like an "insider." Bureaucratic, formal language alienates the user and signals that the app is an "outsider."
